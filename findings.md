# Findings Summary

1. **Development status makes a big difference.** Developed countries average 79.2 years of life expectancy versus 67.11 for developing countries, a gap of about 12 years. The gap stayed similar across years (e.g. 76.8 vs 64.62 in 2000).

2. **Schooling and life expectancy move together.** Countries with the highest schooling (Australia 20.04, New Zealand 18.87, Iceland 18.16) also have life expectancy above 81. Education likely goes along with better healthcare and income, though this shows correlation, not cause.

3. **High GDP does not always mean the longest life.** Qatar has the third highest average GDP (40748) but life expectancy of only 77.03, lower than Switzerland (82.33) at the top.

4. **Low immunization coverage appears with very low life expectancy.** The lowest values in the under-50% coverage group are Sierra Leone (39.0), Malawi (43.5) and Zimbabwe (44.5).

5. **High HIV/AIDS burden is linked with low life expectancy.** Swaziland, Zimbabwe, Lesotho and Malawi have the highest HIV/AIDS death rate and life expectancy around 49 to 51 years.
